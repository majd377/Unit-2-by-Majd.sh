Two success stories (and what we can learn from them)

Education Ministers who are hoping to improve their school systems often look at Finland and Taiwan. The first is a small country in northern Europe with a cold climate and a language hardly anyone else speaks; the second is a very small island that until fairly recently had high levels of poverty and population. Yet today both of these countries come out at or near the top in international surveys of economic success. Year after year, they also score highly in educational results. Can this be an accident, or is there a connection?


Finnish Education Minister Tuula Haatainen certainly believes there is. 'In Finland, we believe we have to invest in education,' she says. One reason she offers for Finland's success is that students don't begin 'real' school until they are 7, and all students between 7 and 16 get the same education. 'We don't divide at an early stage between students who do well and those that don't manage so well,' she explains. 'Studies show that it is dangerous to divide too early into different educational paths.'


Like Finland, Taiwan has nine years of compulsory education, after which (in both countries), students choose either an academic or vocational path. Very few choose to leave school in either country. One important difference, however, is in national tests, which are very important in Taiwan but much less so in Finland.


Can other countries learn from the Finnish and Taiwanese experience? There are surely some lessons that can be learnt as long as we remember that just applying one country's system to another isn't a simple solution to all problems.

Activity 2 Textbook Solutions (True / False / Doesn't Say)

1. The writer suggests that the economic success of Finland and Taiwan is rather surprising.

TRUE

Explanation: Paragraph 1 mentions the difficult climate in Finland and past poverty in Taiwan, making their economic success surprising.

2. The writer believes there is a connection between education and economic success.

TRUE

Explanation: The passage explicitly connects high academic performance with national economic strength.

3. Tuula Haatainen thinks clever students should have special lessons.

FALSE

Explanation: She clearly states that all students between 7 and 16 receive the exact same education without early division.

4. The education systems in Finland and Taiwan have more differences than similarities.

DOESN'T SAY

Explanation: The article discusses similarities and differences but does not state which outweighs the other.

5. The writer says that other countries should copy the education systems of Finland and Taiwan.

FALSE

Explanation: The final sentence warns against simply applying one country's system to another.

Period 2: Grammar Rules (Hope & Wish)
👨‍🏫 القاعدة (Period 2):
• Hope: تُستخدم للحديث عن أهداف وطموحات في المستقبل أو توقع ما نريد حدوثه (أو عدم حدوثه).
   1. hope + to + infinitive للطموحات والأهداف.
   2. hope + subject + verb للتعبير عما نريده في المستقبل (الفعل يكون في صيغة المستقبل will / won't / going to أو صيغة المضارع البسيط Present tense).
• Wish: تُستخدم للتعبير عن رغبة في تغيير واقع حالي موجود الآن.
   1. wish + Past Simple للتعبير عن تغيير الواقع الحاضر.
   2. الماضي من can هو could بعد wish.
   3. فعل to be بعد wish يمكن أن يكون was أو were.

Textbook Examples (أمثلة الكتاب الوزاري):

  • Education Ministers are hoping to improve their school systems ...
  • I hope it won't happen.
  • Let's hope he gets the grades he needs.
  • I wish our school had better equipment.
  • The headteacher wishes the school could buy more computers.
  • I wish I wasn't/weren't so bad at maths.
  • "I hope it doesn't rain tomorrow."
  • "I wish I understood this question."

Activity 1: Complete the Grammar Rules (حل النشاط الأول من الكتاب)

1. We use hope + the infinitive (to + verb) form of the verb to talk about aims or ambitions.

2. We use hope + subject + verb to say what we want to happen (or not happen) in the future.

3. The verb after hope can be in a future form (will, won't, going to) or the present tense.

4. We use wish + the Past Simple tense to say what we want to change about a present situation.

5. The past form of can is could.

6. After wish, the verb to be can be either was or were.

Activity 2: Complete the sentences with the correct form of the verbs in brackets

1. I hope I get / will get good marks in the test tomorrow. (get)

Reason: After 'hope' for future wants, we use Present Simple (get) or Future Simple (will get).

2. She wishes she didn't have so much homework to do. (not/have)

Reason: 'wish' + Past Simple (didn't have) to change a present fact.

3. We don't invest enough in primary education. I hope we spend / will spend more in future. (spend)

Reason: After 'hope' for future wants, we use Present Simple (spend) or Future (will spend).

4. After leaving university, I hope to become a teacher. (become)

Reason: 'hope + to + infinitive' for ambitions and aims.

5. I don't know what he's saying. I wish I could understand French. (can)

Reason: The past form of 'can' is 'could' after 'wish'.

6. Most parents of young children hope they do / will do well at school. (do)

Reason: After 'hope' for future expectations, we use Present Simple (do) or Future (will do).

Top 5 Tips for a Good CV:

Tip / Advice Matched Reason / Continuation
1. Keep it short (2 pages maximum): they don't want your life story.
